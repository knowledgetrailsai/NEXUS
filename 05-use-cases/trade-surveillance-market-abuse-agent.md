# Trade Surveillance & Market Abuse Detection Agent

`Home › 05-use-cases › Trade Surveillance & Market Abuse Detection Agent`

**Exclusive domain:** [Capital Markets & Investment Banking](../03-domains/capital-markets-investment-banking.md)
**Primary Function:** [Risk & Internal Audit](../02-functions/risk-internal-audit.md)

## Who This Is For

- **In plain terms:** regulators require firms to watch every trade for signs of manipulation or misconduct — insider trading patterns, spoofing, front-running. Today that watching is done by surveillance analysts working through alerts generated by rules-based systems that produce a lot of noise. This agent synthesizes the alert, the trader's history, and the relevant market context into a single evidence-backed case file, so the analyst spends their time on genuine judgment calls instead of chasing down context by hand.
- **For business readers:** surveillance quality is a direct regulatory and reputational exposure — a missed pattern can mean enforcement action, and an under-resourced alert queue (most firms' reality) means analysts triage by time available, not by actual risk. Compressing the evidence-assembly step lets the same analyst team cover more ground with the same headcount, which is usually the actual constraint, not analyst judgment itself.
- **For technical readers:** this means read-only access to order and trade data, market data feeds, trader communications metadata (where in scope and compliant), and the documented market-abuse indicator library — producing a structured case file per alert with the full evidentiary trail, never an automated disposition (cleared/escalated) decision.

## Problem / Trigger

Rules-based surveillance systems generate a high volume of alerts, most of which require an analyst to manually pull trading history, market context, and related communications before they can even begin assessing whether the alert reflects genuine misconduct — a time-consuming step that delays review and contributes to alert backlogs.

## Agentic Automation Opportunity

When a surveillance rule fires, the agent assembles the relevant trading history, market context (price/volume movement around the event), and any other alerts involving the same trader or instrument, and produces a structured case file summarizing the pattern with full evidence citations — routing it to a surveillance analyst for disposition. The agent never clears or escalates a case itself.

## Workflow Boundary

**Trigger and inputs:** A fired surveillance rule/alert from the existing rules engine; trader ID, instrument, and time window.

**Systems and evidence consulted:** Order and trade management system (read-only), market data feed, prior alert history for the same trader/instrument, and — where in scope and compliant — trader communications metadata.

**Outputs and allowed actions:** A structured case file with evidence citations, submitted to the analyst queue. No disposition action (clear, escalate, report) is taken by the agent.

**Human owner / approval point:** Surveillance analyst reviews every case file and makes the disposition decision; compliance officer owns any regulatory reporting decision.

**Exception paths:** Missing or incomplete trade data → flag the gap explicitly rather than presenting an incomplete case as conclusive; a pattern involving instruments or communications outside the agent's authorized data scope → route with a note on the scope limitation rather than silently omitting it.

**Out of scope:** Case disposition, regulatory reporting decisions, and any action on a trader's account or trading privileges.

## Automation Maturity

- **Realistic starting level:** L2 — agent assembles and synthesizes every fired alert into a case file, analyst disposes of every case
- **Potential ceiling:** L2, as a near-permanent posture — case disposition is a regulatory and legal judgment that should not be automated regardless of pattern-matching maturity; the achievable gain is in evidence-assembly speed and completeness, not disposition autonomy
- **Why this range:** The cost of an incorrect autonomous disposition (clearing a genuine violation, or escalating and damaging a client/trader relationship without cause) is severe enough that this function should stay assist-level even at full maturity.

_Indicative scale:_
`L1 (assist) — L2 (automate, reviewed) ●○ — L3 (automate, exception-routed) — L4 (autonomous, monitored)`

## Value Signal

- **Plausible outcome definition:** A surveillance alert resolved with a complete, evidence-backed case file assembled without manual data-gathering by the analyst.
- **Plausible value basis:** Time saved (analyst case-assembly hours), risk reduced (faster, more complete review reducing the chance of a missed genuine violation in the alert backlog).

## KPIs Influenced

- **Leading:** Case-file assembly time per alert, alert-backlog size and age, evidence-completeness rate on assembled cases.
- **Lagging:** Analyst throughput (cases disposed per period), time-to-disposition, regulatory examination findings related to surveillance timeliness or completeness.

## Evaluation Plan

- **Offline test set:** Historical alerts with known dispositions, covering both genuine violations and false positives across instrument types and abuse patterns.
- **Quality measures:** Evidence-completeness accuracy, case-file usefulness to analyst (measured via analyst feedback), time saved versus manual assembly baseline.
- **Launch gates:** Zero cases presented as complete when evidence is actually missing; analyst-rated case quality meets or exceeds manually-assembled baseline before scaling alert volume covered.
- **Rollout path:** Shadow assembly alongside manual process → assist for a subset of alert types → expand coverage as validated.

## Implementation Notes

- **Data dependencies:** Order/trade management system, market data feed, prior alert history, and the documented market-abuse indicator library.
- **Integration dependencies:** Read-only API access to trading systems and market data; case-management system integration for queue submission.
- **Permissions / audit requirements:** Strictly read-only on trading systems; full evidence trail retained per case file, including data sources and time windows used.
- **Likely failure modes:** Incomplete data access creating a false impression of thoroughness; stale or delayed market data producing an inaccurate context picture; communications-metadata scope creep beyond what's authorized.
- **Open discovery questions:** What communications data sources are in scope and compliant to include? How does the case file format align with existing regulatory reporting templates? What's the escalation path for a case involving data outside the agent's authorized scope?

## Key Risks & Guardrails

- Case disposition is a compliance and legal judgment that must remain human-owned regardless of how mature the pattern-matching becomes
- An incomplete case presented as thorough is worse than no case at all — the agent must explicitly flag data gaps rather than implying completeness
- Communications-data scope must be tightly bounded to what's legally authorized for surveillance use — this is a materially higher-sensitivity data category than most in this repository
